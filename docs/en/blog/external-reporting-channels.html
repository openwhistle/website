External reporting channels: when employees may go straight to an authority

Which external reporting channel is responsible in Germany, its deadlines, when public disclosure is protected, and what that means for employers.

27 October 2026 Updated 27 October 2026 4 min read OpenWhistle Contributors

In Germany, employees may choose freely: the employer's internal reporting office, or an authority's external reporting office (§ 7 Abs. 1 HinSchG) [1]. They should prefer the internal one where it can act effectively and no reprisal is to be feared. There is no duty to report internally first.

Not legal advice

This article gives general information. It reflects the HinSchG as amended on 25 September 2026 [1].

The reporting routes

Which external reporting channel is responsible

External office Responsible for Provision
Federal Office of Justice (BfJ) [2]everything no other external office covers§ 19 Abs. 4
BaFin [3]financial supervision, such as banks, insurers and securities trading, and money laundering where BaFin is the supervisor§ 21
Bundeskartellamt [4]competition law and the Digital Markets Act§ 22 Abs. 1
A federal state, if it has set one upits own state and municipal administration§ 20

For the Bundeskartellamt the law says so explicitly: a report is possible at any time, whatever the outcome of an internal procedure (§ 22 Abs. 1).

What the external office does

Step Deadline Provision
Confirm receiptpromptly, within 7 days at the latest§ 28 Abs. 1
Check whether the breach falls under the lawno deadline of its own§ 28 Abs. 2
Give feedbackwithin 3 months, 6 months in extensive cases§ 28 Abs. 4

The external offices also advise before a report, and point to the internal route when they do (§ 24 Abs. 2).

When going public is protected

Whoever makes information public is protected only under the conditions of § 32. The usual case: after an external report, no follow-up or feedback came within the deadline. Exceptions apply, for example in an emergency or where evidence could be destroyed.

What this means for employers

  • Inform. The internal reporting office provides clear information on the external procedures (§ 13 Abs. 2).
  • Make the internal route attractive. Employers should create incentives for it, but external reporting must not be made harder (§ 7 Abs. 3).
  • Do not obstruct. Obstructing a report risks a fine of up to €50,000, for a company up to €500,000 (§ 40 Abs. 2 Nr. 1, Abs. 6 with § 30 Abs. 2 Satz 3 OWiG).
  • Act quickly and visibly. An internal office that confirms, follows up and takes measures is the best reason to report internally.

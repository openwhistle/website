When do you need a whistleblowing system? The obligation under the HinSchG

A whistleblowing system is mandatory from 50 employees, in finance from the first. A decision tree, the edge cases and what happens without a reporting office.

27 October 2026 Updated 27 October 2026 4 min read OpenWhistle Contributors

In Germany you need a whistleblowing system if you normally employ at least 50 people [1]. Certain financial companies need one whatever their size. The Whistleblower Protection Act (HinSchG) does not say “whistleblowing system”: it says internal reporting office with reporting channels (§ 12, § 16 HinSchG).

Not legal advice

This article gives general information. It reflects the HinSchG as amended on 25 September 2026 [1]. For your own case, ask a lawyer.

Do you need a whistleblowing system? Check in 30 seconds

Financial companies include credit institutions, insurers, investment firms, asset management companies, and crypto and payment institutions. The full list is in § 12 Abs. 3.

Who counts as an employee

The law looks at the number normally employed (§ 12 Abs. 2). It does not mention full-time equivalents. Employees under § 3 Abs. 8 are:

Who Provision
Employees§ 3 Abs. 8 Nr. 1
Trainees and others employed for vocational training§ 3 Abs. 8 Nr. 2
Civil servants, judges, soldiers§ 3 Abs. 8 Nr. 3–5
Persons similar to employees, including home workers§ 3 Abs. 8 Nr. 6
People working in sheltered workshops for disabled people§ 3 Abs. 8 Nr. 7

An employer is any person or organisation that employs at least one person: companies, partnerships, associations, foundations and public authorities (§ 3 Abs. 9).

Edge cases

Case What applies Provision
A group with several companiesThe threshold applies to each company separately. A third party may take on the tasks.§ 12 Abs. 2, § 14 Abs. 1
Several companies with 50 to 249 employees eachA shared office is allowed. Remedy and feedback stay with each company.§ 14 Abs. 2
Federal and state governmentThe supreme authorities choose the units that need a reporting office.§ 12 Abs. 1
Municipalities and their companiesWhether and from when is a matter of state law.§ 12 Abs. 1
Fewer than 50 employeesNot obliged. Employees can still turn to an external reporting office, such as the one at the Federal Office of Justice.§ 7 Abs. 1, § 19

The threshold of 50 comes from EU Directive 2019/1937 (Art. 8(3)) [2]. After a risk assessment, member states may also oblige smaller companies (Art. 8(7)).

Since when, and what happens without a reporting office

What Since Provision
Obligation from 250 employees and for financial companies2 July 2023§ 12
Obligation for private employers with 50 to 249 employees17 December 2023§ 42 Abs. 1
Fine of up to €20,000 without an internal reporting office1 December 2023§ 40 Abs. 2 Nr. 2, Abs. 6; § 42 Abs. 2

What the reporting office must offer

  • Two channels. Reports in writing and orally, in person on request (§ 16 Abs. 3).
  • Access only for those responsible (§ 16 Abs. 2).
  • Two deadlines. Confirm receipt within 7 days, give feedback within 3 months (§ 17).
